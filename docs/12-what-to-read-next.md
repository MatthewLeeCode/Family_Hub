# 12. What to read next

Family Hub works out each person's taste from the books they have **finished**
and the books they have **rated**, and uses it in two places.

Nothing happens until a person has finished or rated **five books**. Before
that there is too little to go on, and both places say how many more are
needed.

## In the library you already have

**Discover → In the library: similar to what I've read** shows the twelve
unread books in the library that match best, closest first. Each card says why:

- **More by** an author you rated well;
- the tags the book shares with books you enjoyed.

How it weighs things:

- A book you rated 4 or 5 counts for its author and tags. A book you rated
  1 or 2 counts against them. A book you finished without rating counts a
  little in favour.
- A tag on a handful of books says more than a tag on half the library, so
  "Gothic" outweighs "Fiction".
- Every sort of tag is used: book file, community, family and your own.

Books you have started, finished or rated are left out.

This view is only as good as the tags. A library where few books have tags
gives a short list; see [guide 10](10-tags.md).

## Books the library does not have

**Discover → Suggested for you this week** lists up to twelve books that are
not in the library:

- more books by the authors you rate highest, and
- well-liked books from the last 35 years on the subjects you rate highest.

Each has a reason, **Find it** (fills in the book search; only for people
who may find new books) and **Not for me** (the book is never suggested to
you again).

A new list is made once a week. **Suggest again** makes one now.

Things to know:

- The list comes from Open Library. What is sent is an author's name or a
  subject, never who is asking.
- It is switched off together with tag lookups: **Admin → Hub → Connections → Community tags from book catalogues → Look up tags for new
  books**.
- Your list is yours. Nobody else sees it, admins included.
- Subjects are matched by name, so the occasional suggestion is wide of the
  mark: a liking for the family tag "Pining" can bring up a book about pine
  trees. Use **Not for me**.
- Family tags that mean something only at home ("Read together") match
  nothing outside and are skipped.
